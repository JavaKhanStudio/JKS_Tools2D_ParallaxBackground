What this lab shows

Candidate cloud shaders (r556), written as they would ship (GLSL ES 1.0) and drawn by the real reader compiled to JavaScript, in the three settings Ficelle needs first: a Mediterranean day, a night in a cold forest and a medieval fantasy evening. None is in the library yet. WISPS (streaks, the one picked), LOBES (cut-paper cumulus: round lobes heaped on a flat foot, each keeping its outline as a painter draws one) and PUFFS (soft cumulus, set aside) are made in the shader, nothing is painted, so a page needs no cloud art; they are lit from where the sun or moon stands in the picture, in two colours a page would store (the lit and the shaded), which is all that changes from day to night. WISPS as cumulus is WISPS under other numbers (short, heaped on a flat base): the same shader. Painted, relit is a page's painted clouds under BILLOW (a slow warp of their pixels), their colours replaced by the same two: OneNight's cumulus, and calm's bank. Painted, still is what a page does today: the painting under the page's tint. The view picks LOBES over the painted cumulus in each setting, one setting under every kind, WISPS both ways, or PUFFS; a click on a panel zooms it over the stage. The sliders of the made clouds scale each setting's own numbers, so one move shows in every setting; light turns the sun or moon round the sky, and the clouds' lit side follows; toon steps cuts the shade in flat bands. Copy settings gives every slider as JSON for the card.
