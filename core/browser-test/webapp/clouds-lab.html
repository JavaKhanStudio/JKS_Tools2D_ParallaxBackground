What this lab shows

CUMULUS (r556, round 3) is a cloud shader written as it would ship (GLSL ES 1.0) and drawn by the real reader compiled to JavaScript. It is not in the library yet. Nothing is painted: a page needs no cloud art, the clouds tile, and they are lit from where the sun or moon stands in the picture, between two colours a page would store (the lit and the shaded), which is all that changes from day to night. Each panel is a whole sky of three layers of that one shader (a far bank on the horizon, a middle, the near clouds), each at its own size, speed and haze, in a view as tall as it is wide, as a phone held upright sees a page: a Mediterranean day, a night in a cold forest, a medieval fantasy evening, its sun low so the clouds' bellies are the lit side. The view puts one setting under the shader's three looks, which are numbers of it: CUMULUS (painted: a shade in three soft tones), cut in flat tones (a drawn look) and soft (a frayed, softer edge). Earlier rounds keeps what rounds 1 and 2 showed: WISPS, a page's painted clouds churned and relit, and the painting still under the page's tint, which is what a page does today. A click on a panel zooms it over the stage. The CUMULUS sliders scale each layer's own numbers, so one move shows in every setting; light turns the sun or moon round the sky, and the clouds' lit side follows. Copy settings gives every slider as JSON for the card.
