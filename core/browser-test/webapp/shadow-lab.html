What this lab shows

r365's AI forest page (the hills lab's, its hills as they ship), drawn twice by the real reader, side by side: A under the hour's light, with cast shadows; B as painted. The hour moves the sun and the moon: the sun rises at 6 at the view's left, is noon height up at 12 and sets at 18 at its right; the moon follows the same path, later by its moon age (full: it rises as the sun sets). Play 24 hours runs a whole day and night in day length seconds, and the moon ages a day. The light comes from the measured clear-sky day (docs/daylight.md): about 100,000 lux under a high sun, 600 at sunrise, 3 at the end of dusk, 0.27 under a full moon, a thousandth on a moonless night. The world's brightness follows those lux on a log scale, raised to the dusk curve, from darkest night to full from full light from degrees up. Its colour is the sun's (red-gold low down), the sky's (blue) and the moon's, each by its share; at night, as eyes see in the dark, grey blue (night blue). Warmth scales how much of that colour shows. A cast shadow lacks its body's direct light and keeps the sky's: at noon it is about 90% of the light, at sunrise almost none (darkness 1 is as measured). You can drag the sun in the sky (in either panel) to take it off its path; the hour puts it back. A sun in front of you lays the shadows toward you, so each layer casts on the layer just in front of it, lowered by the depth between them times the tangent of its height. As the camera scrolls, a shadow moves with the layer it lies on. Rays: the sun's light past the ridges in front of it, in A. Sun behind you takes the sun and the moon out of the sky, behind the camera: their shadows lie on the ground away from you, and the relief lights each layer's flanks turned toward them. A lab, not an effect yet. The view without the pines shows the hills' shadows whole.
