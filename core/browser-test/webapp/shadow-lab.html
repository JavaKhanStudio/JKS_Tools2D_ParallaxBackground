What this lab shows

r365's AI forest page (the hills lab's, its hills as they ship), drawn twice by the real reader, side by side under the same sun: A with cast shadows, B as painted. The sun is in the sky you see, behind every layer: drag it there (in either panel), or set its side and height with the sliders; under the horizon it is gone behind the mountains. A sun in front of you lays the shadows toward you, so each layer casts on the layer just in front of it: the far peaks on the range, the range on the hills, the hills on the pines. The pines, in front of everything, cast on nothing you see. A ridge's shadow is its whole mass, lowered by the depth between the two layers times the tangent of the sun's height: what rises above it is lit, the rest is in the shade. A low sun shades most of each layer, a high one little; once it has set, every layer is in the shade. As the camera scrolls, a shadow moves with the layer it lies on (with the layer it falls on 1, as in the world). A second sun casts its own shadows; where two meet, it is darker. A lab, not an effect yet: what ships, if any of it, waits for your tests. The view without the pines shows the hills' shadows whole.
