What this lab shows

r365's AI forest page (the hills lab's, its hills as they ship), drawn twice by the real reader: A as painted, B with the pines casting their shadow on the hills just behind them, and on nothing else: not on the sky, not on the layers further back. Each light is a sun: a side round you (behind you, on your left or right, or ahead in the picture) and a height over the horizon, set by its sliders or by dragging it on the dial over B's corner (seen from above: you at the bottom, the picture at the top, overhead in the middle, the horizon at the rim; the line from the middle shows where its shadows point). Sunlight comes from above, so a shadow lands below and away from the sun: on hills distance behind the pines, a low sun from behind your left lays it long to the right, a high one drops it behind the trees. A sun ahead casts toward you, onto nothing behind the pines. As the camera scrolls, the shadow moves with the pines (it stays under its tree) or with the hills (as the world would: the trees, nearer, slide past it). Where two suns' shadows meet, it is darker.
